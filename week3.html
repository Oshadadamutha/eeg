Week 3: ThinNet

Designing Spatio-Temporal Filters for Brain Signals

The Philosophy: Constraints Breed Creativity

To combat the massive overfitting seen in Week 2, I didn't need a bigger model; I needed a smarter one. I researched architectures like EEGNet and DeepConvNet and designed a custom variant called thinNet.

The goal was to decouple Temporal Learning (frequency patterns over time) from Spatial Learning (which part of the brain is active).

Architecture Deep Dive: thinNet.py

I replaced standard square kernels (e.g., 3x3) with rectangular kernels to force the network to learn specific types of features.

Layer 1: The Temporal Filter

# Layer 1: Learn Frequencies (Alpha/Beta waves)
# Kernel: (1, 11) -> Height 1 (1 channel), Width 11 (time samples)
# This acts like a Bandpass Filter, ignoring spatial info.
nn.Conv2d(in_channels=1, out_channels=12, kernel_size=(1, 11), stride=(1, 1))

This layer slides only across time, effectively learning to spot specific brainwave frequencies (like the 8-12Hz Mu rhythm associated with motor movement) independently for each electrode.

Layer 2: The Spatial Filter

# Layer 2: Learn Spatial Patterns
# Kernel: (2, 11) with Stride (2, 1)
# This combines adjacent electrodes to detect WHERE the signal is coming from.
nn.Conv2d(in_channels=12, out_channels=24, kernel_size=(2, 11), stride=(2, 1))

This layer mixes information from neighboring electrodes. By strictly defining these dimensions, I reduced the trainable parameters by ~70%.

Regularization Strategy

I also introduced aggressive regularization layers directly into the model definition:

  1. Dropout (p=0.5): Randomly zeroing out 50% of neurons during training to prevent co-adaptation.
  2. BatchNorm: Normalizing layer inputs to stabilize the gradient descent.

Outcome

The thinNet architecture converged faster and the gap between Training and Validation accuracy narrowed significantly. The model was no longer just memorizing noise; it was forcing itself to find structural patterns in the alpha/beta frequency bands.
