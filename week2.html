Week 2: The Baseline Trap

Overfitting on High-Dimensional Noise

The Objective

With data flowing into the GPU, the next step was to establish a "Baseline." In software engineering, this is the Minimum Viable Product (MVP). I needed to prove that a neural network could learn anything from this data before optimizing architectures.

The Baseline Architecture: convNet

I implemented a standard Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) similar to VGG-16 but adapted for the rectangular shape of EEG data.

  • Input: 1 x 12 x 2500
  • Layers: 6 Convolutional Layers, Max Pooling, ReLU activation.
  • Loss Function: CrossEntropyLoss (for 6-class classification).
  • Optimizer: Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation).

The Overfitting Phenomenon

The results were a classic lesson in the "Curse of Dimensionality."

  • Training Accuracy: ~99.9% (The model memorized the data).
  • Validation Accuracy: Stalled at ~25%.

Because the input vector was so large (30,000 data points per sample) and the dataset size was relatively small (hundreds of trials), the standard convNet simply memorized the Gaussian noise inherent in the EEG signals. It wasn't learning features; it was acting as a lookup table. This failure was critical—it proved that a generic "computer vision" approach wouldn't work for biological signals. I needed a specialized architecture.
